# yPrompt — App Store Metadata v1.0.5

Goal of this version: hand back a finished clip, not a raw recording. After each take the reader watches it back with the silence trimmed and captions spelled like their script, then saves it or records again. It's all part of Pro (the camera already is), so it adds to what a trial shows.

**Ship only after 1.0.4 has a clean trial-to-paid readout** from the Nov 7–10 check, so the two versions can be told apart in App Store Connect.

Rules followed below:
- Same terms and tone as 1.0.4: "textes"/vous (fr), "Skript"/du (de), "guion" (es), "copione" (it), "roteiro" (pt-BR), "naskah"/Anda (id).
- es-ES writes "vídeo", es-MX "video"; fr-CA uses the fr-FR text.
- These translations need a native speaker's review before upload.

## CHANGES IN THIS BUILD (1.0.5)

**Takes**
- Recordings no longer go straight to Photos. When recording stops, a full-screen Take sheet plays the take in a loop with Save to Photos, Share and Retake.
- Retake asks first, deletes the take and moves the script back to where the take began. The X discards the take, also after asking.
- Takes are kept in the app's own folder (not backed up) until they're saved or thrown away. A take left unsaved, for example because the app closed, opens again the next time its script is opened. A recording that never finished is removed.
- The camera keeps running under the sheet, so Retake can start straight away.

**Trim Silence** (on by default, remembered)
- Cuts from 0.3 s before the first word to 0.5 s after the last. Without recognized words, it uses the take's loudness against the room's own noise. Takes under 1 s after trimming aren't cut.
- Saving with only a trim copies the video without re-encoding.

**Captions** (Pro; Lyrics by default; style and position remembered)
- After a take, its sound is run through Apple's speech recognition (on the device when possible) in pieces of up to 50 s, and the words are lined up with the script. Captions use the script's spelling and punctuation; a short misheard stretch between two matching words becomes the script's words; ad-libs keep what was heard; skipped script words are left out.
- Captions break at the script's phrases and pauses, up to 5 words (7 to finish a phrase), and ad-libs get captions of their own.
- Styles: Lyrics (the line waits dimmed and each word lights up and grows slightly as it's said) and Clean (white bold with a shadow). Position: Bottom or Middle.
- The preview and the saved video are drawn by the same code, as a Core Image video composition. Saving with captions re-encodes in HEVC when the take is HEVC.
- Share includes a `.srt` subtitle file when captions are on.
- If speech recognition isn't allowed, the sheet says so with a Settings button, and trimming still works from loudness.
- Free users whose Pro has ended can still save and trim their takes; captions show a lock.

**Rotate**
- A Rotate button on the take turns it a quarter clockwise per tap, for takes that came out sideways. Captions are laid out for the turned frame. Saving with captions turns the frames; without captions only the orientation tag changes, so nothing is re-encoded.

**Recording orientation**
- A take started with the phone lying flat no longer comes out sideways. Flat on a table the phone can't tell which way is up, so the take now follows the screen's orientation then. Held upright, it still follows the horizon as before.

**Other**
- The screen no longer dims or locks while the script plays, while recording, or while the take sheet is open.
- The Pro feature list has a Captions row.
- What's New on iPhone and iPad shows Captions, Review Every Take and Lyrics Style Captions. The Mac keeps the 1.0.4 sheet.
- The speech recognition permission text mentions captions.
- Debug: `-ypDemoTake <video file>` opens a video as a take in the simulator, which has no camera.

**Not tested yet**
- Everything was run in the iPhone simulator with generated clips (a computer voice reading the welcome script, portrait and landscape). Not yet on a real device: a real camera take, front and back camera, 4K/60 and HDR saves, Voice Scroll sharing the mic with recording, render time on older iPhones, and a non-English script.
